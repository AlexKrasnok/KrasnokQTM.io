Openings & Contact

Join or Collaborate with QTM Lab

QTM Lab welcomes motivated researchers interested in quantum photonics, metamaterials, quantum sensing, cryogenic quantum systems, and computational electromagnetics.

How to ApplyContact Information

Prospective Ph.D. students, postdocs, and undergraduates

We welcome students with backgrounds in electrical engineering, physics, optics, and computing. Research projects combine theory, simulation, and experiments, with training tailored to each student's experience.

Research Areas

  • Complex-frequency electrodynamics and non-Hermitian scattering
  • Quantum control, circuit-QED, and cryogenic quantum interfaces
  • Metamaterials, metasurfaces, antennas, and nanophotonics
  • Quantum sensing, MRI metasurfaces, and applied metrology

Working Style

  • Reproducible Python/Jupyter workflows
  • Versioned code, traceable data, and careful notebooks
  • Simulation, measurement, and theory connected by evidence
  • Strong technical writing and publication-quality figures

How to Apply

Send a CV, transcript if applicable, a short research statement, and representative publications, code, or technical writing if available.

Availability depends on funding, project fit, preparation, timing, and mentoring capacity.

Email: akrasnok@fiu.edu

Working Together

We aim to make the lab a place where people can ask questions, discuss setbacks openly, and enjoy doing research. Mentorship includes support for careers within the group and after members move to new positions.

Develop Your Ideas

Students help shape their projects and receive regular feedback on calculations, experiments, and manuscripts. We discuss results together and work through difficult problems.

Support One Another

Questions and concerns are welcome. We share technical knowledge, discuss problems early, and respect colleagues' time and contributions.

Make Research Reproducible

Organized notebooks, shared code, and documented measurements help us build on one another's work and make our findings useful to other researchers.

Learn Experimental Skills

New lab members receive guidance on equipment and measurement methods. Experimental training includes the FIU safety requirements for the work being performed.

Find a Research Direction

Early meetings focus on the literature, the student's interests, and a manageable first project. Research plans develop as the work progresses.

Prepare for the Next Step

We support fellowship applications, conference presentations, and professional connections. Career discussions reflect each person's goals in academia, industry, or national laboratories.

Research and Funding

Prospective researchers can contact Prof. Krasnok to discuss their interests, preparation, and possible projects. Openings depend on project needs and available support.

We help students develop fellowship proposals and connect their research plans with suitable funding programs. Students from all countries are welcome to inquire; eligibility for individual awards varies.

Student Support

Research assistantships, university fellowships, and external awards can support graduate study. Funding arrangements are discussed individually during recruitment.

Fellowships and Proposal Mentoring

Students interested in quantum photonics, metamaterials, quantum sensing, cryogenic systems, or computational electromagnetics can contact Prof. Krasnok for help developing a fellowship application.

Application dates below are current as of September 26, 2026. Each program links to its sponsor's full eligibility criteria and application instructions.

Open Competitions

NSF GRFP (2027 competition)

Deadlines: October 20 for computing and materials; October 22 for engineering; October 23 for physics and mathematics. All are at 8 p.m. Eastern. Reference letters are due October 16 at 8 p.m. Eastern.

Three years of support for eligible research-based graduate study. Applicants must be U.S. citizens, nationals, or permanent residents and meet the current undergraduate or first-year graduate eligibility rules. Second-year graduate students are ineligible.

NSF GRFP 2027 solicitation

NDSEG Fellowship (FY2027)

Deadline: October 30, 2026, at 5 p.m. Eastern.

Three-year graduate fellowships for defense-related science and engineering. Applicants must be U.S. citizens or nationals and be applying to, or near the beginning of, an eligible U.S. graduate program.

NDSEG application and eligibility

Hertz Fellowship (2027)

Deadline: October 30, 2026.

Up to five years of support for Ph.D. study in applied sciences, mathematics, or engineering. Primarily for college seniors, first-year graduate students, and applicants preparing to enter graduate school; U.S. citizenship or permanent residency is required.

Hertz Fellowship application

DOE SCGSR (2026 Solicitation 2)

Deadline: November 4, 2026, at 5 p.m. Eastern.

Support for 3–12 months of thesis research at a DOE laboratory. Applicants must be U.S. citizens or permanent residents, have advanced to Ph.D. candidacy, and develop a project with a DOE laboratory scientist in an eligible research area.

SCGSR key dates

GEM Fellowship (2027)

Deadline: November 13, 2026.

Graduate funding and paid industry or national-laboratory internships through GEM university and employer partners. Open to eligible U.S. citizens and permanent residents with a minimum 3.0 GPA; a participating university must confirm graduate admission and funding support.
