Research

We work on wave physics for quantum technologies: how the poles and zeros of a resonant system, and its electromagnetic environment, set what a device can do. We test this with theory, simulation, and measurements on microwave, photonic, and superconducting hardware.

  • Complex-frequency electrodynamics and non-Hermitian scattering
  • Quantum control and quantum-hardware diagnostics
  • Circuit and cavity QED and photonic interfaces
  • Nanophotonics, metasurfaces, and reconfigurable antennas
  • Quantum sensing, cryogenic sensing, and applied metrology
  • Computational electromagnetics and quantum simulation

Complex-frequency electrodynamics and non-Hermitian scattering

The poles and zeros of a resonator lie off the real frequency axis, so a steady tone sees only part of its response. We drive resonators with signals that grow or decay in time. Such complex-frequency excitations give virtual absorption, scattering beyond steady-state bounds, and full 2π phase control at constant amplitude.

Quantum control and quantum-hardware diagnostics

Qubits share lines, cavities, and packages, so a pulse meant for one qubit also reaches its neighbors. We design waveforms that load only the chosen circuit mode, with less reflection and crosstalk, and diagnostics that include the real electromagnetic environment of a device.

Circuit and cavity QED and photonic interfaces

Superconducting cavities and qubits store and process quantum information at millikelvin temperatures. We study cavities, resonators, and couplers with better isolation and bandwidth, metamaterials for cryogenic hardware, and wireless links that carry signals out of the cold stages.

Nanophotonics, metasurfaces, and reconfigurable antennas

Dielectric resonators, metasurfaces, and polaritonic crystals shape light, microwaves, and near fields with structures smaller than the wavelength. Our work ranges from all-dielectric nanoantennas to topological polaritons in twisted van der Waals bilayers.

Quantum sensing, cryogenic sensing, and applied metrology

We design sensors for cryogenic stages, MRI scanners, and aging infrastructure: diamond and atomic magnetometers for bridge inspection, metasurfaces that shape the radio-frequency field in 3 T MRI, and a cryogenic dielectric antenna for wireless sensing outside the 10 K stage.

Computational electromagnetics and quantum simulation

Full-wave electromagnetic solvers, circuit models of scattering anomalies, and QuTiP and Qiskit models of quantum systems connect our designs with measurements. NVIDIA awarded the lab 32,000 A100 GPU-hours in 2026 for this work.
